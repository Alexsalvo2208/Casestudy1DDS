# Casestudy1DDS
Employee attrition analysis and prediction using R, exploratory data analysis, KNN, and Naive Bayes classification models.

#Executive Summary 

Employee attrition presents a financial and operational challenge for Frito Lay. The objective of this analysis was to identify factors significantly associated with employee turnover, develop classification models to predict attrition, and evaluate potential replacement costs. The dataset contained 870 employees and 36 variables, with 140 employees (16.1%) leaving the company.
Exploratory data analysis identified overtime, job involvement, and monthly income as three key factors associated with attrition. Employees working overtime had an attrition rate of 31.8%, compared with 9.7% among those who did not. Employees with the lowest job involvement had an attrition rate of 46.8%, compared with 8.6% among those with the highest involvement. Additionally, employees who left earned an average monthly income of 4,765 dollars , compared with 6,702 dollars for those who stayed. Chi-square tests and a Welch two-sample t-test provided sufficient statistical evidence to suggest significant associations or differences in means for these variables (p < 0.001).
Two classification models, K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) and Naive Bayes, were developed using eight predictors: OverTime, JobInvolvement, JobRole, StockOptionLevel, WorkLifeBalance, MonthlyIncome, YearsAtCompany, and Age. The data was divided into 70% training and 30% validation sets. On the validation data, KNN achieved 67.8% accuracy, 63.4% sensitivity, and 68.6% specificity. Naive Bayes performed better, achieving 77.8% accuracy, 73.2% sensitivity, and 78.6% specificity. Both models exceeded the target of 60% sensitivity and specificity, and Naive Bayes was selected as the preferred model.
Additional findings showed that job role, stock option level, age, and years at the company were also significantly related to attrition, while years since last promotion was not statistically significant (p = 0.898). Financial analysis estimated employee replacement costs between $28,589 and $228,709 per employee, based on an average annual salary of $57,177 among employees who left. Providing a $200 retention incentive to the 77 employees flagged by Naive Bayes in the validation set would cost $15,400, although the effectiveness of this intervention remains unknown.
Overall, the findings suggest that Frito Lay should focus on reviewing overtime demands, improving employee engagement, and evaluating compensation competitiveness. The Naive Bayes model provides a potential tool for identifying employees at risk of leaving and supporting more targeted retention decisions.
